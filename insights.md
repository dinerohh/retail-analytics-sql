# Insights for Duka Smart Electronics

Figures are in KSh and come from `queries.sql`. Money figures use Completed orders only. Data runs from January 2025 to 30 September 2026. Overall, 634 completed orders brought in KSh 24,155,372 revenue and KSh 2,890,922 profit, a margin of about 12 percent.

## 1. Accessories fund the business (Q16, Q33, Q42)
Accessories are only 4.5 percent of revenue (KSh 1,077,496) but deliver KSh 456,746 profit, or 15.8 percent of the total, at a 42.4 percent margin, while every other category earns between 9.9 and 11.4 percent. This matters because a shilling of accessory sales is worth about four shillings of phone or TV sales in profit, even though Mobile Phones lead revenue at KSh 6.94 million. I recommend bundling an accessory with every phone, laptop and TV sale and tracking accessory attach rate as a monthly target.

## 2. Discounts are causing losses (Q33, X1, X2)
62 completed orders (9.8 percent) were sold below cost, losing KSh 141,979, mainly on Hotpoint Refrigerators (KSh 38,716), iPhone 13 (KSh 29,008) and laptops. Orders with no discount earn a 17.5 percent margin, but orders with more than 10 percent discount earn negative 0.9 percent on KSh 3.12 million of revenue. This matters because these big-ticket products only carry 4 to 6 percent margins, so almost any discount erases the profit. I recommend capping discounts on refrigerators, TVs and iPhones at about 3 percent and requiring manager approval above 10 percent on anything.

## 3. Nairobi dominates, while Mombasa and Machakos are under-used (Q25, Q17)
Nairobi produces KSh 9.58 million, or 39.7 percent of revenue, followed by Mombasa at KSh 3.16 million. Machakos is the smallest city but its three buyers spend the most each (KSh 248,367), and Thika buyers spend the least (KSh 116,358). This matters because heavy reliance on one city is risky, and high spend per customer in Mombasa and Machakos signals room to grow. I recommend recruiting more customers in those two towns and running accessory bundles in Thika to lift spend per customer.

## 4. VIP customers spend more, but Retail is most of the revenue (Q27, Q28)
Average spend per buying customer is KSh 253,252 for VIP, KSh 197,959 for Corporate and KSh 163,575 for Retail, yet Retail brings in KSh 14.39 million, or 59.6 percent of revenue, because there are 88 of them. VIP customers order Home Appliances most often (28 orders). This matters because moving a Retail customer up to VIP behaviour adds roughly KSh 90,000 each. I recommend a clear Retail-to-VIP upgrade path with perks focused on appliances and phones rather than price cuts.

## 5. A third of buyers have gone quiet (Q34, Q23)
47 of 132 buying customers (35.6 percent) have not ordered in over 90 days, and they account for KSh 4.60 million, or 19.0 percent, of lifetime revenue, including VIP Janet Korir (KSh 593,882, 114 days inactive) and Corporate customer Diana Kiprop (KSh 529,880, 98 days). A further 12 registered customers have never ordered. This matters because winning back known customers is cheaper than finding new ones. I recommend personal calls to the lapsed customers above KSh 200,000 this month, and a small accessory offer to the 12 who never bought.

## 6. Growth is strong but highly seasonal (Q38, Q39, Q40, Q31)
Revenue for January to September 2026 is KSh 12.83 million, 86.3 percent above the same months of 2025, with August 2026 the best month at KSh 2.24 million. December 2025 jumped 59.7 percent on November to KSh 2.03 million, then January 2026 fell 84.1 percent to KSh 322,433, the lowest month in the data. This matters because the swing strains stock and cash, with lost sales in December if understocked and tied-up cash in January. I recommend building stock for November and December, planning a January accessory promotion, and budgeting cash for the January dip.

## 7. One in five orders never completes (Q20, X3)
11.25 percent of orders are cancelled (KSh 3.47 million of order value) and 9.5 percent are still pending (KSh 3.18 million), with cancellation highest on M-Pesa (12.5 percent) and cash (12.1 percent) and lowest on bank transfer (4.8 percent). This matters because about KSh 6.6 million of order value has not become revenue, and M-Pesa carries 55 percent of orders. I recommend taking a deposit or full payment at order time for M-Pesa and cash, and following up on any order pending longer than seven days.

## 8. A small group carries the business (Q45, Q41)
Using the 25th and 75th percentiles of spending among buyers (KSh 35,201 and KSh 252,835), 33 customers are High Value and they generate 69.2 percent of revenue, while the top five alone (led by Brian Chebet at KSh 848,119) contribute 15.8 percent. This matters because losing even a few of these customers would be costly. I recommend assigning an account contact to every High Value customer and reviewing their activity monthly so a drop in orders triggers a call before they lapse.
